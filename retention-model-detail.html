Student Early-Warning Retention Model

A dropout-prediction model built to catch risk early enough for advisors to actually act on it — not confirm it after the fact.

The Situation

UTC's retention office relied on lagging indicators — end-of-term grades — that surfaced far too late for proactive advising. OPEIR's existing retention tracking was entirely manual: isolated SQL queries and Excel merges rebuilt from scratch for every request, with no standing early-warning system in place.

The goal was an automated, reproducible pipeline that standardizes messy institutional data, filters noise, trains a predictive model, and outputs a ranked at-risk roster advisors can actually use — before a student's academic record has already told the story.

The Model That Caught Its Own Mistake

The first trained model hit a suspiciously strong 0.928 ROC-AUC and 0.82 F1 — a result that looked great until I traced it back to two variables: Final_Inst_GPA and Final_Deans_List_Ind. Both are end-of-term outcomes — using them to predict dropout is hindsight bias, not prediction: a symptom of already having dropped out, feeding the model the answer.

The correction
0.928 → 0.917 ROC-AUC

I stripped every end-of-term variable and rebuilt the feature set around genuinely early, actionable signals — high school absences, suspension history, and credit-earning velocity. The score dropped slightly. The model became honest.

For deployment, catching at-risk students matters more than a clean precision number — so I re-optimized the Logistic Regression decision threshold from 0.50 down to 0.25. That traded some precision (66.37%) for 90.93% recall, missing only 37 of 408 actual dropouts instead of 84 — a 56% reduction in students who would have fallen through the cracks.

Statistical Rigor: Feature Interrogation

Before training, I ran a full statistical pass to catch multicollinearity rather than trusting feature selection to the model alone — Point-Biserial correlation, Cramér's V, Mutual Information, and Pearson correlation, each matched to the right variable-type pairing.

01

Built Cramér's V from a lower-level primitive

Used scipy.stats.chi2_contingency as the mathematical basis for a custom Cramér's V implementation, rather than a pre-packaged function.

02

Dropped redundant, multicollinear features

College (0.999 Cramér's V duplicate of Major_Group), a reading-score field (0.78 Pearson with English score), and a college-count field (0.70 Pearson with major count) were all dropped after the interrogation surfaced the overlap.

03

Chose class weighting over synthetic oversampling

class_weight="balanced" handled the ~43% imbalanced dropout rate mathematically rather than generating synthetic minority-class rows (e.g., SMOTE) — a defensible, explainable choice.

04

Constrained the Decision Tree deliberately

max_depth=6, min_samples_leaf=25 — explicit overfitting prevention, forcing generalizable rules rather than hyper-specific edge cases.

Business Insight

The Financial Aid Paradox

Applying for financial aid correlates with increased dropout risk (a proxy for financial stress), while being awarded aid is protective. A counterintuitive, genuinely actionable finding — translated into a plain-language executive summary for non-technical university leadership, not left buried in model coefficients.

The dominant protective factor across both models was Cum_Earn_Rate (credits earned vs. attempted) — it alone drives 83.2% of the Decision Tree's split decisions. Exploring multiple majors also showed up as protective rather than risky, contrary to the intuitive "indecision is a red flag" assumption.

Honest Limitations

Worth stating plainly rather than glossing over:

  • The model is trained on cross-sectional terminal snapshots, not longitudinal data — even with leakage removed, it functions more as a "diagnostic profile" than a pure Day-1 prediction. A methodological ceiling, not a hidden flaw.
  • No micro-behavioral data (LMS logins, campus app usage) is included yet — the model is entirely macro/academic-record based.
  • Cross-validation hasn't been run yet — currently a single stratified 80/20 split. Upgrading to stratified 5–10 fold CV is the next required step before this can support a published manuscript.
