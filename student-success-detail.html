The Situation

UTC had no unified student-level or student-term analytical framework. Enrollment, degree, advising, and tutoring data were siloed with inconsistent student identifiers across systems — and the official census enrollment flag was entirely null, meaning there was no reliable way to confirm who was actually enrolled in a given term.

Worse, prior ad-hoc retention analyses had real, uncaught errors baked in: a broken denominator that inflated retention to 1.0, inconsistent term ordering, and — most consequentially — graduated students were being counted as attrition, artificially depressing the institution's own reported persistence rates. OPEIR needed a rebuilt, standardized system, not another one-off report.

What I Built

A single pipeline (run_pipeline.py, 23 sequential scripts) that regenerates every research table and figure from raw administrative dumps — replacing isolated SQL queries and Excel merges rebuilt from scratch for every request. Four disjoint ID systems (institutional ID, UTCID, the advising platform's student ID, and username) were standardized into one master student_id.

Since the official enrollment flag was null, I built an independent workaround: attempted credit hours > 0 as the enrollment proxy, encoded as a single vectorized condition. On top of that panel, five independent analytical modules answer the five research questions, all sharing the same cohort definitions and term indexing — so results are comparable across reports for the first time.

Two Errors That Were Distorting the Numbers

Fixed before launch

Graduates were being counted as dropouts

Rebuilt the retention logic so graduating students are correctly excluded from ongoing attrition denominators — a fix that directly changes how "healthy" the institution's retention rates actually look, not a cosmetic adjustment.

Caught late-stage, before publication

A cumulative metric that went up

The 2020 transfer cohort's cumulative retention curve ticked upward between terms 4 and 5 — mathematically impossible for a cumulative metric. Root-caused via a full code audit, fixed, then re-validated every other cohort file for the same class of bug before the report went out.

Findings, By Research Question

RQ1

Cumulative Retention

FTIC 2019 cohort (N=2,310): 78.1% retained at 1yr, declining to 57.7% at 3yr. Transfer cohorts drop off faster — 76.6% at 1yr to just 6.2% at 4yr for the 2019 cohort.

RQ2

Stop-outs

931 stop-out events across 888 unique students, 60.7% involving exactly a 1-term gap. Stop-out rate fell sharply over time — FTIC 2019 at 8.2% down to FTIC 2023 at 1.1%.

RQ3

Pre-college credit and graduation

2019 FTIC cohort: students entering with pre-college credit graduated at 65.8% vs. 38.0% without — a ~28-point gap, consistent across the 2020 and 2021 cohorts.

RQ4

Transfer graduation by cohort

3-year graduation rates ranged from 48.6% to 52.1% across 2019–2022 transfer cohorts, using a purpose-built academic-year cohort grouping ("Oscar's Rule": Summer + Fall + following Spring entrants).

RQ5

First-year engagement and 4-year graduation

Students engaged with advising/writing-center support in year one graduated at 57.1% vs. 42.1% for non-engaged students — a 15.0-point completion gap directly tied to first-year engagement.

Honest Limitations

  • All results are descriptive and associational, not causal — the 15-point RQ5 engagement gap can't be attributed to advising/tutoring alone, since it doesn't control for motivation, help-seeking behavior, or prior preparation. No significance testing was run on this or any other gap.
  • No predictive or ML model was built — this stayed strictly descriptive. The pipeline's own documentation flags survival analysis of time-to-degree as the natural next step.
  • Summer term dynamics are entirely excluded — the pipeline only indexes Fall/Spring.
  • Recent cohorts have truncated observation windows, forcing suppression of later-checkpoint metrics for the newest transfer cohorts — a structural limitation of cohort research, not a pipeline bug.
